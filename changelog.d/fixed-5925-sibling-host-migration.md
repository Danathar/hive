- Documented the constraint that makes a public-hostname move an ordered operation rather than a rename, and pinned it in the hub test suite ([#5925](https://github.com/hivecommons/hive/issues/5925)). First-party sibling products have no login of their own: `dibs` resolves a visitor by forwarding the browser's `hive_hub_user` cookie to the hub's `/api/saas/whoami` ([#4171](https://github.com/hivecommons/hive/issues/4171)), which works only while the browser sends that cookie to the sibling — and it does so solely because the cookie is scoped to the registrable domain of the hub's own canonical host. The hub and the sibling must therefore share a registrable domain. When the hub became `hive.hivecommons.dev` on 2026-09-04 the session cookie became `Domain=.hivecommons.dev`, and `dibs.kubestellar.io` stopped receiving it; the symptom is not an error but a sibling that renders every visitor as signed out. Moving `dibs` under `hivecommons.dev` is what repairs it. Operators get a new **Moving a public hostname** runbook in the migration tracker covering that coupling, the Let's Encrypt issuance cap the re-issue has to wait for, and the wildcard's actual coverage (`*.hive.hivecommons.dev` does **not** cover a name one level up such as `dibs.hivecommons.dev`); the `HIVE_HUB_PUBLIC_URL` reference now warns that changing its registrable domain signs users out of siblings left on the old one. The runbook also records why the retired host must serve a redirect instead of continuing to serve the application: a browser ignores a `Set-Cookie` whose `Domain` does not cover the host that sent it, so a hub on `hivecommons.dev` can never scope a session to `kubestellar.io`, and no configuration exists that would let it — the redirect is the mechanism, not a preference.
